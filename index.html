What is AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)?

AEO, or Answer Engine Optimization, is the practice of making a page, a brand or a person the source that an AI answer engine selects and cites when it answers a question. When a user asks ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google AI Overviews who holds the title, the engine does not return ten links. It resolves the entity, picks a handful of sources it trusts and writes one answer. AEO is the work of being one of those sources, ideally the first.

Answer Engine Optimization grew out of SEO but rewards different things. SEO optimizes a page to rank; AEO optimizes a passage to be quoted and an entity to be recognized. A page that opens with the answer, states its date, defines its terms and is corroborated elsewhere on the web is easy for an LLM to use. A page that warms up for four hundred words, the way classic SEO content often does, is not.

AEO vs SEO vs GEO vs DEO

The vocabulary around answer engines is crowded, and the four labels are used interchangeably by people who should know better. They are not the same thing.

TermGoalUnit optimizedMeasured by
SEO (Search Engine Optimization)Rank on a results pageThe pagePosition, clicks
AEO (Answer Engine Optimization)Be the cited source in an AI answerThe passage and the entityCitations, entity resolution
GEO (Generative Engine Optimization)Visibility across all generative AI outputsThe brand's footprintMentions, share of voice

AEO sits in the middle of that table on purpose. It needs SEO fundamentals underneath it, technical SEO in particular, it feeds the broader GEO footprint above it, and DEO is what AEO becomes when the question is "which one should I choose" instead of "what is this".

How to do AEO: the 5-step method

This is the method behind every page in the portfolio and behind this page. It is short because the discipline is not complicated; it is consistent, and it sits on ordinary SEO fundamentals.

  1. Answer in the first sentence. The page targets one question, exactly as an SEO page targets one keyword, and sentence one is the answer. Engines quote pages that commit; they skip pages that build up to it.
  2. Date the claim. "As of" plus a date turns an opinion into a record. LLMs prefer current, dated statements when two sources disagree.
  3. Make the entity unambiguous. This is entity SEO. Same name, same title, same description on the page, in the schema graph and on every profile linked from sameAs. Answer engines trust entities they can pin down.
  4. Get corroborated off the page. One site saying something is a claim. A press release, a LinkedIn post, a video and three independent mentions saying the same thing is a fact an engine will repeat. Corroboration is the step most guides leave out.
  5. Publish at news speed and re-check. Ship the answer the day the question appears, then ask the engines the question every week and log what they return. What they return is the only metric that matters.
