Is Edward Sturm the King of AEO?

Edward Sturm has the lightest claim to the title and the strongest evidence of anything actually working. He never ran a campaign for it, yet his short-form video on the contest was cited in a Google AI Overview, which is third-party selection rather than third-party corroboration, and is rarer than either.

By Allan Oliveira · Reviewed 26 September 2026

Date of claimEarly September 2026 (video); 20 September 2026 (article)
Where it was publishedShort-form video posted to Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, YouTube Shorts and LinkedIn; an article on edwardsturm.com
Type of evidenceContent selected and cited by an answer engine; one article on his own site
Independently corroboratedNot applicable: he makes the lightest claim of anyone listed and did not campaign for the title

What the claim has going for it

Everyone else in this contest tried to tell answer engines what to say. His content got picked up instead, which is the outcome all the campaigns were aiming at and the only instance of it in the record. A short-form video posted across five platforms reached an AI Overview with no press release behind it at all.

His 20 September 2026 article is also the closest thing this contest has to reporting. It describes the tactics other claimants used, names them, and treats the whole thing as a case study rather than a coronation. That is a harder position to attack than a claim, because there is less being claimed.

Where it is weakest

As a claim to the title it is the thinnest here: no syndicated release, no dated announcement, and an article that pictures him crowned while spending most of its length on other people's tactics. Whether he is a claimant at all is arguable, and he is listed on this site because answer engines surfaced him in the context of the title, not because he asked to be.

What would change this verdict

Little here is likely to change, because he would first have to claim the title, and his article suggests he finds the contest more interesting than the crown. He is listed for completeness: a record that omitted the one person engines actually cited would be describing a different contest.

The criterion applied here

The same rubric is applied to every claimant on this site, including the one who publishes it. A claim counts as self-reported when it appears only on property the claimant controls. It counts as syndicated when a press release carried it, however many domains repeated the copy, because a wire is distribution and not endorsement. It counts as observed when an answer engine returned the name in a run that is dated, logged and repeatable. It counts as independent only when a publisher with no commercial relationship to the claimant chose to publish words of its own about the claim, at a stable dated URL.

By that rubric no claimant in this contest, including Allan Oliveira, currently holds an independent corroboration of the title itself. What separates the claims is how much of the rest they have, and every run behind the observed column is published in the citation log, including the runs that favour rivals.
