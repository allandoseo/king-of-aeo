How do you get cited by ChatGPT?

You get cited by ChatGPT when it searches the web for your query and finds a passage worth attributing, which is narrower than it sounds: in a fixed set of logged runs it answered several questions from its own knowledge and cited nothing at all. The work is to be the clearest available source on queries that do trigger a search.

By Allan Oliveira · Reviewed 26 September 2026

There is no submission form, no index to join, and nothing you can buy. What exists is a retrieval step that sometimes runs, and a model that decides whether any retrieved source is worth naming.

SituationWhat happensWhat you can influence
It answers from its own knowledgeNo sources shownAlmost nothing in the short term
It searches and finds youYour page named as a sourcePassage clarity, entity resolution, corroboration
It searches and finds othersCompetitors namedBeing the more usable source next run
It declines to pick oneSeveral names listed, no single answerBeing in the list, which is itself an outcome

Half the work is knowing when it searches

In the runs recorded here, ChatGPT answered two of four questions with citations attached and two entirely from its own knowledge, with no sources at all. Those are different games. On the second kind, no amount of on-page work changes the answer this week, and the honest response is to measure rather than to optimize blindly.

It does discount networks

One result in the log is worth more than most advice on this subject: asked who holds a contested title, ChatGPT listed several claimants and explicitly discounted one whose supporting sites shared a publisher. The model noticed that eight domains saying the same thing were one voice, not eight. Building a network to manufacture agreement is a strategy that the thing you are trying to persuade can already see through.

What being a usable source looks like

A page that states the answer plainly, dates it, says what it does not cover, and keeps its identifiers consistent is cheap for a model to attribute. A page that requires the model to interpret, reconcile or defend it is expensive, and there is always a cheaper page. The page-level work is set out separately.

Expect variance, and plan for it

The same prompt returned different answers minutes apart in the same session. That is not a bug to be defeated, it is the property of the system, and it is why a single screenshot proves nothing and a series proves something. The measurement method is built around exactly that.

The logged-out session is a different system

Measure while signed out, always, and know what that buys and costs. It removes memory, personalization and saved context, which is the only way two runs a week apart are comparable. It also means you are seeing what a stranger sees, which is the population that matters. What it does not give you is the experience of a logged-in customer with history, and no honest report pretends otherwise.

Being listed is not being chosen

Asked who holds a contested title, the model has repeatedly declined to pick one and listed several claimants instead. That is a real outcome and it deserves its own row: appearing among four names is neither a win nor a loss, and a report that scores it as either is hiding the finding. The useful question over a series of runs is whether the list is getting shorter and whether you are still on it.

What no amount of work changes

You cannot make the model search when it has decided it does not need to, and you cannot make it cite when it is composing from memory. Those weeks look like failure in a report and are not. This is the argument for measuring coverage, how many of the planned observations produced an answer at all, as a first-class number rather than a footnote.
