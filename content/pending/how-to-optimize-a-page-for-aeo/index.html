How do you optimize a page for AEO?

You optimize a page for AEO by putting the answer in its first sentence, dating and scoping that answer, and making every supporting fact checkable without leaving the page. This is page-level work and it is different from the site-level sequence: here the unit is the passage an engine will lift, not the entity it will resolve.

By Allan Oliveira · Reviewed 26 September 2026

Site-level AEO decides whether a machine knows who you are. That sequence is covered separately. This page is about the smaller unit: one page, one question, one passage worth lifting.

The first sentence is the whole game

An answer engine composing a reply is looking for a span of text that answers the question and can stand alone. A page that opens with context, then background, then finally the answer in paragraph four has made itself expensive to use. Write the claim, then qualify it. The reader loses nothing; the machine gains everything.

Dated claims survive being quoted

An undated claim quoted six months later is a claim the publisher can be held to and did not intend. Putting the date and the scope inside the sentence solves this and has a side effect worth more than the protection: engines reproducing a dated statement tend to reproduce the date, which makes your claim traceable back to you.

Structured data is confirmation, not decoration

Markup that describes something the page does not say is the fastest way to get the page discounted. The useful pattern is narrow: mark up the entity, the question, the answer and the dates, and make every value identical to the text a reader sees.

Then check whether it worked

Page-level changes are testable in the only way that counts, by asking the question and recording what comes back. The log published here does that for a fixed set of prompts, and the rest of this cluster covers the method and the comparisons.

What to remove

Three things cost you extractions and all three read as good writing in a different context. Preamble, because a paragraph of throat-clearing pushes the answer out of the liftable span. Unscoped superlatives, because "the best approach" cannot be reproduced by a system that has to stand behind what it says, while "the approach that worked in these three runs" can. And sentences that depend on the paragraph above them, since a passage lifted alone loses its antecedent and becomes unusable.

The test is mechanical and takes a minute. Copy any paragraph out of the page, read it cold, and ask whether it still answers something. If it needs the page around it, it will not be quoted, however well it reads in place.

The steps, in order

  1. Answer in the first sentence Open with the claim itself, in a sentence that survives being read alone, with no preamble before it.
  2. Date and scope the claim Say when it was true and where it applies. "As of September 2026, in the United States" is what an engine reproduces.
  3. Put the evidence next to the claim Each factual statement gets its source in the same passage, so lifting the passage does not strip its support.
  4. Use one heading per question A heading that is the question a person typed makes the passage beneath it addressable.
  5. Mark up what the page already says Structured data confirms the visible text. It does not add facts, and it must not contradict them.
  6. Re-check on a schedule Put the review date on the page and keep it honest, because a stale answer about a moving system is a wrong answer.
