How do you rank in Google AI Overviews?

You do not rank in a Google AI Overview, because it is not a ranked list: you get cited by one, or you do not appear at all. Getting cited means having a passage that answers the query plainly, an entity Google can resolve, and enough corroboration that the answer is safe to attribute to you.

By Allan Oliveira · Reviewed 26 September 2026

The framing in the question is the first thing to correct, and it is not pedantry. Ranking is a position in a list of links. An AI Overview is composed text with a handful of attributed sources. There is no position two. You are cited or you are absent.

What the same query returned in two countries

On 26 September 2026, logged out and without personalization, the query "king of aeo" returned three claimants in Brazil and one in the United States. Both screenshots are published on the main record. The sources cited differed too. Anyone optimizing for "the" AI Overview is optimizing for something that does not exist as a single object.

What people optimize forWhat the Overview actually does
A positionComposes an answer and attributes a few sources
One global resultDiffers by country, and by session
Appearing for a keywordAppears only on queries that trigger it at all
TrafficOften answers without a click, and citation is the visible outcome

Citation follows resolution

In the runs recorded in the citation log, the Overview resolved the entity in ten of twelve observations, meaning it treated the subject as a known thing rather than repeating the query string back. That is the gate. A page whose subject the engine cannot resolve does not get attributed, however well the passage reads.

One practical warning about measuring this

The Overview renders after the rest of the results page. In one logged run it was absent at eight seconds and present at ten. Anyone checking quickly will record "no AI Overview for this query", which is a strong claim and would have been false. Wait, and confirm an absence twice before writing it down.

What does not work

Volume does not. In the same record, a claimant supported by at least eight matching domains under one publisher was not returned by the Overview in any logged run, while a short-form video with no distribution behind it was cited. The surface rewards being a usable source more than it rewards being a loud one.

What to record when you check

Five columns, and the fifth is the one people leave out. The date, the exact query, the country and language, whether an Overview appeared at all, and whether your domain was among the attributed sources. Without the fourth column you cannot distinguish "we were not cited" from "there was nothing to be cited in", and those call for completely different responses.

Being cited is not the same as being visited

An Overview frequently answers the question outright, and the citation is a name rather than a click. Measuring this channel by sessions will show you very little; measuring it by whether you were named, and by whether the engine resolved you as a known entity, shows you what actually changed. The protocol is written down precisely so that the columns stay the same between runs.

The steps, in order

  1. Confirm the query even triggers one Many queries return no AI Overview at all. Check before optimizing for a surface that does not appear.
  2. Write the answer as a liftable passage One question, one plain answer, stated before any qualification.
  3. Make the entity resolvable Consistent name, description and identifiers, so Google can attribute the passage to a known thing.
  4. Get corroborated off your own domain Overviews lean on sources that agree with each other, and self-published claims agree only with themselves.
  5. Re-check by country The same query returns different names in different locales, so a result in one market says nothing about another.
